# `places.csv` - Lexicon of Mythological Places

## Purpose

This lexicon aims to provide a curated list of significant mythological locations, realms, and geographical features. This includes otherworldly realms (heavens, underworlds, paradises), legendary cities, sacred mountains, enchanted forests, and other named places that feature prominently in myths. Its purpose is to:

1.  **Identify and Locate (Symbolically):** Provide a unique identifier and name for each mythological place.
2.  **Facilitate Narrative Mapping:** Allow users to trace journeys or understand the cosmological setting of myths.
3.  **Support Analysis:** Enable study of how different cultures conceptualize space, sacred geography, and the relationship between the mundane and mythical worlds.

## Structure

The `places.csv` file will ideally contain the following columns:

*   `place_id`: A unique, stable identifier (e.g., `PLC_OLYMPUS_GRK`, `PLC_ASGARD_NOR`, `PLC_XIBALBA_MAY`).
*   `preferred_name`: The most common or academically preferred name (e.g., "Mount Olympus", "Asgard", "Xibalba").
*   `culture_group`: The primary mythological or cultural tradition(s) associated with the place.
*   `place_type`: A broad classification (e.g., "Divine Realm", "Underworld", "Mythical Island", "Sacred Mountain", "Legendary City", "Cosmic Region").
*   `description_brief`: A short (one sentence) description of the place or its significance.
*   `inhabitants_associations`: Key deities, creatures, or events associated with this place.
*   `cosmological_location`: If applicable, its position within the culture's cosmology (e.g., "Celestial", "Chthonic", "Earthly but hidden").
*   `alternate_names`: Other common names or variations.
*   `notes`: Additional contextual information or important attributes.

## Curation Process

This list is **not predefined or exhaustive** and will be built iteratively:

1.  **Ingestion-Driven:** New places added as identified in texts.
2.  **Scholarly Referencing:** Based on mythological atlases, encyclopedias, and academic studies of sacred geography.
3.  **Scope:** Focus on places with specific mythological names and significance, rather than generic locations (e.g., "a forest" vs. "Mirkwood"). Distinguish between mythical places and real-world locations that feature in myths (though the latter might be included if their mythical significance is paramount).

This approach will develop a rich gazetteer of the mythological world(s) described in the codex.

# Sources

Every text in this archive is a public-domain translation. This file records where each one came from — translator, edition, year, and the URL it was sourced from — so that anyone can trace a passage back to its origin and check it. The authoritative, machine-readable version of this same information is [`Corpus Forge/corpus_manifest.json`](Corpus%20Forge/corpus_manifest.json); each work's YAML frontmatter carries it too.

Twenty-five works are bundled as full text. Two more (the Shan Hai Jing and the Huarochirí Manuscript) are tracked as desired additions but **not** included, because a lawful public-domain English text has not been located for them.

## Notes on rights

Most of these are old enough that their public-domain status is not in question — a translation published in 1898 is plainly out of copyright. Three deserve a word.

**§ Táin Bó Cúailnge.** This is Faraday's 1904 translation of the older, shorter Recension I (from *Lebor na hUidre* and the Yellow Book of Lecan) — the first English rendering of the epic. It is a *partial* witness: Faraday abridged some of the long rhetorical passages, and her bracketed `[Note: ...]` insertions mark gaps in the manuscript. An earlier build of this archive carried Cecile O'Rahilly's 1967 translation of the fuller Recension II; that text was removed because O'Rahilly died in 1980 and her translation remains under copyright. The fuller recension is a candidate for re-inclusion once a public-domain text of it exists.

**‡ The Poetic Edda.** Bellows' translation was *first published in 1923* by the American-Scandinavian Foundation, which places it in the public domain in the United States. The sacred-texts edition is dated to a later (1936) printing, but the 1923 first edition is the one that governs. It is now also on [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/73533).

**† The Pyramid Texts.** Mercer's translation was published in 1952 — recent enough that its status is not automatic. It is public domain in the United States because the copyright was never renewed (renewal would have been due around 1980, and no record exists), and because the work was published simultaneously in the U.S. and Canada, the 1996 GATT restoration of foreign copyrights does not reach it. This is also the basis on which sacred-texts.com hosts it. If you are outside the U.S., check your local term rules before redistributing this one.
